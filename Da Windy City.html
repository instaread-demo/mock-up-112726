Jon Gruden isn't buying the hype surrounding the Bears' winning streak

ByChris Pownall|Nov 26, 2026, 11:00 AM EST

Five consecutive wins haven't convinced Jon Gruden that the Chicago Bears have everything figured out offensively.

The former NFL head coach has been impressed with what the Detroit Lions have accomplished in recent weeks, particularly on defense. However, during Wednesday's episode of the Gruden Family Football Show, Gruden expressed concerns about Chicago's offense heading into its Thanksgiving matchup with Detroit.

“I worry about the Bears a little bit offensively,” Gruden said. “They've been turning the ball over. It hasn't been pretty offensively.”

It's an interesting assessment considering how much attention the Bears have received during their winning streak. Chicago has found ways to win football games, but Gruden isn't necessarily convinced that its recent offensive performances are sustainable against better competition.

Jon Gruden questions the Bears' offensive production

Perhaps the most concerning part of the discussion was that Jon wasn't alone in his assessment.

Former Washington head coach Jay Gruden questioned how Chicago's receivers looked on film, specifically pointing to Rome Odunze's movement and Luther Burden III's struggles to create separation.

“They look slow on tape for whatever reason,” Jay said of the Bears.

Neither coach expressed the same concerns about Chicago's defense. In fact, both were complimentary of what Jeffery Simmons has brought to the unit.

The offensive criticism, however, is particularly relevant given Ben Johnson's background and the expectations that accompanied his arrival in Chicago.

Winning five consecutive games has understandably overshadowed some of those concerns. Had the Bears lost a couple of those games, the conversation surrounding their offensive production might look considerably different.

Detroit presents an opportunity to put those concerns to rest.

According to Jon, the Lions have held four consecutive opponents to 275 yards or fewer and 21 points or fewer. Both Gruden brothers ultimately picked Detroit to win on Thanksgiving.

Caleb Williams could be the difference against Detroit

Despite his concerns, Jon acknowledged that Chicago has a quarterback capable of changing the outcome.

“To me, the Bears win this game if Caleb Williams can scramble around and make some plays,” Gruden said.

Williams' ability to extend plays is an obvious advantage for Chicago, but Johnson would presumably prefer an offensive performance that doesn't require his quarterback to routinely improvise.

That's what makes Thursday's matchup particularly intriguing. The Bears have won five straight, while Detroit enters the game having won six consecutive contests.

Chicago has already demonstrated that it can win without playing its best offensive football. Beating Detroit on the road would go a long way toward quieting the concerns raised by both Gruden brothers.

At some point, though, the Bears need their offensive production to be as convincing as their record.
