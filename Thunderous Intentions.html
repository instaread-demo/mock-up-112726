Thunder set to get back exactly what they've been missing with Alex Caruso return

ByMark Nilon|Nov 25, 2026, 9:00 AM EST

Though it may feel like the OKC Thunder are adding new players to the injury report with every passing game this season, heading into Wednesday's bout against the Minnesota Timberwolves, for the first time in a long time, they're actually taking someone off of it.

After a three-game absence due to a hip impingement sustained early in last week's loss to the Hawks, Alex Caruso will finally be making his return to the hardwood.

While getting any player back in the fold should be exciting given how depleted Oklahoma City's active roster has been, Caruso, in particular, could give them exactly what they've been missing over these past few games: perimeter defense.

Dating back to their 117-112 loss to Atlanta, a game where they let up a whopping 20 made triples, the Thunder have allowed opposing teams to shoot a ridiculous 40.9 percent from beyond the arc, the sixth-worst mark in the association over this span.

To put this into perspective, they've seen teams average roughly 50 points from deep per game alone.

It's no coincidence that this is all happening without Caruso available, as the veteran's an established All-Defensive talent who, this season alone, has been holding opponents to just 29.3 percent shooting from distance per 100 possessions.

Especially considering the T-Wolves are the fourth-best three-point shooting team currently in the association (boast a 37.5 percent clip), his expertise is being reintroduced to OKC's rotation at the perfect time.

Thunder heading into much-needed home game amid losing streak

Not only do the Thunder get a core rotation player like Alex Caruso back during their upcoming face-off against the Timberwolves, but they'll also be heading home for the first time since last Friday.

Despite the hardships they've faced both in the health department and in the record books, OKC has remained dominant while playing out at Paycom Center this season.

Over a string of seven games played at home, the Thunder have gone 6-1, tied for the third-best record in the association. In comparison, when playing on the road, they've gone just 4-7.

It's no wonder the Thunder have gone just 2-6 over their last eight games, as six of them were played on the road, including their three-game losing streak from November 10-13, marking their first stretch of three-straight losses in over two years.

Now, amid their latest losing streak of two straight, the Thunder finally get the luxury of heading back to the Sooner State, where they hope to keep their home-court edge.
